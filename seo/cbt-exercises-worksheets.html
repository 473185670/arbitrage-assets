The 10 Cognitive Distortions

Cognitive distortions are systematic thinking errors that trigger and maintain anxiety, depression, and stress. The first step to breaking them is naming them. Here are all 10:

1. All-or-Nothing Thinking

Seeing things in black-and-white categories. No gray area.

"If I don't get a perfect score, I'm a total failure."

2. Overgeneralization

One negative event = a never-ending pattern.

"I failed this interview. I'll never get a job."

3. Mental Filter

Picking out a single negative detail and dwelling on it exclusively.

"They said one critical thing" (ignoring 9 positive ones).

4. Disqualifying the Positive

Rejecting positive experiences as if they don't count.

"That compliment was just them being polite."

5. Jumping to Conclusions

Mind reading (assuming others' thoughts) or fortune telling (predicting the worst).

"They didn't text back → they hate me."

6. Magnification / Minimization

Exaggerating the bad. Shrinking the good.

"A minor mistake = catastrophic. A real achievement = no big deal."

7. Emotional Reasoning

"I feel it, therefore it must be true."

"I feel anxious, so something bad must be about to happen."

8. Should Statements

Rigid rules about how you/others "should" be. Creates guilt and resentment.

"I should never make mistakes. I should always be productive."

9. Labeling

Attaching a fixed, negative label to yourself instead of describing the action.

"I made an error" → "I'm an idiot."

10. Personalization

Taking responsibility for events outside your control.

"My friend is in a bad mood → it must be something I did."

The Thought Challenging Worksheet (5 Minutes)

  1. Trigger: What happened? Just the facts. ("My manager scheduled a 1:1.")
  2. Automatic thought: What jumped to mind? ("I'm going to be fired.")
  3. Distortion check: Which of the 10 is this? (Fortune telling + magnification.)
  4. Evidence FOR the thought: What supports it? Be honest.
  5. Evidence AGAINST: What contradicts it? My last 1:1 went fine. My review was positive.
  6. What would I tell a friend? The compassionate, balanced perspective.
  7. Balanced thought: "My manager wants to check in. I've been performing well. I'll go in curious, not scared."
  8. Re-rate emotion: Anxiety 80 → 25. That drop is the worksheet working.

Why Writing Beats Thinking

Research by Storm & Stone (2018) found that written thought records reduce anxiety 40% more than thinking through the same material mentally. Writing forces specificity — thinking stays vague. A vague thought is hard to challenge; a written thought is concrete and can be examined.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the 10 cognitive distortions?
All-or-nothing thinking, overgeneralization, mental filter, disqualifying the positive, jumping to conclusions, magnification/minimization, emotional reasoning, should statements, labeling, and personalization. The worksheet helps you identify which ones you use most.
How do I challenge a cognitive distortion?
Write the distorted thought, identify which distortion it is, list evidence for and against, ask what you'd tell a friend, and write a balanced alternative. About 5 minutes per entry.
Does CBT work for anxiety?
Yes. CBT is the most evidence-based treatment for anxiety. Daily thought records can reduce symptoms 30-40% over 4-8 weeks.
Can I do CBT exercises on my own?
Yes. Self-guided CBT with worksheets is effective for mild to moderate anxiety. This Notion template provides the structure. For severe symptoms, combine with a therapist.
